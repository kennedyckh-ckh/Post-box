# Group Work: facilitator-led collaborative group activities

A web application for classes, workshops and professional learning. A **facilitator** creates a session and groups; **participants** join with a code, write their own contributions inside their group, the group submits, the facilitator chooses what to share, participants respond to each other, and the facilitator can optionally use AI to help summarise.

The platform works fully **without AI**. AI is an optional layer that only produces private drafts for the facilitator to review.

Four things are kept separate on purpose: who wrote something (**individual authorship**), a group handing work in (**submission**), a stage being available (**release**), and what other groups can see (**publication**).

**Current status:** built and tested as described in `docs/TEST-REPORT.md`. It has **not** been deployed to Render and the live HKU AI connection has **not** been tried. Read “What is and is not verified” below before relying on it.

## What it does

**Sessions.** Create, edit, duplicate (clean copy), archive (read-only), restore, delete permanently (the owner types the exact title). Optional templates: blank (Part 1, 2, 3), discussion, inquiry, case analysis, reflection, project planning. Titles, group names, stage titles, instructions and guiding questions are all editable. Three stages by default; switch one or two off to use fewer. The database holds stages as a collection so more than three are possible later (the interface offers three).

**Joining.** Join code + display name + group (with optional group PIN and seat cap) or facilitator-assigned groups. Optional facilitator-defined fields such as Class and Class Number; nothing school-specific is required. Every participant has a unique private identity, so two people can share a name and typing someone's name never gives access to their work. A private recovery code, or a new one issued by the facilitator, restores access on another device. The code is not shown at joining: participants open “Your recovery code” on their session page whenever they need it (stored encrypted; only their own signed-in device can display it). Refreshing keeps you signed in. Seat caps hold even when people join at the same moment (tested with 12 separate processes racing for 5 seats).

**Stages.** Separate controls: enabled, visible, locked/open/closed, a per-group override, and a per-group “not taking part”. Instructions, several links, images and PDFs, as many guiding questions as the facilitator wants (required or optional), each with an optional heading and optional sub-questions (1a, 1b… shown together with their main question), all editable at any time, each shown to participants or kept hidden until the facilitator reveals it (one at a time with “Reveal next question”, individually, or all at once; also from the projector view), tags, allowed contribution types, and a reference panel showing the group's own earlier-stage work (labelled draft or submitted snapshot).

**Participant work.** Text entries with tags, picture attachments (PNG/JPEG, location data removed), editable data tables, each attributed. Saving is acknowledged by the server before the screen says saved; stale edits are never silently overwritten (a conflict shows both versions and lets you choose); version history is kept. Groups stay in sync by polling every few seconds. An optional jointly written group response sits beside, and never replaces, the individual entries.

**Submission.** A confirmation explains that it locks the stage for the group; required questions are checked; teammates who currently have an editor open are named. The snapshot is taken atomically, records who and when, cannot be created twice by repeated clicks, and the work becomes read-only. A facilitator can reopen one group's submission; resubmitting makes a new version and keeps the old one.

**Facilitator dashboard.** Progress (submitted out of expected groups; empty and left-out groups never block; moving on early needs a typed reason that is recorded), people, groups, stages, contributions, sharing, AI, settings, and a history of important actions.

**Sharing.** Publish a single entry or a group's submitted version as a frozen snapshot; later edits stay private until updated; the facilitator is told when a shared item differs from its source. Group labels or anonymous labels (“Sample Response 3”) with one switch; stopping sharing removes access immediately. A preview shows exactly what participants will see, with a warning that text and pictures can still identify people.

**Timer.** A facilitator-started countdown per session (presets or any length, optional label, pause, resume, +1 minute, reset). Participants see it in a bar at the top of their pages, and the projector shows it in the corner or full screen. It is informational only: nothing locks when time is up.

**Reactions and peer responses.** Configurable reaction types, each with an icon (thumbs up, heart, star, light bulb, clapping hands, smile, question mark or tick; inferred from the label when not chosen) (one of each type per person, repeat clicks harmless, removable), configurable scope (inside groups, on shared items, or both), sortable with a stable tie order and a reminder that counts show engagement, not correctness. Structured peer responses use the facilitator's own prompts, stay beside the original, keep internal authorship, follow the anonymity setting, and can wait for approval.

**Projector view.** Opened from the session page in its own window (signed in as the facilitator) and made full screen: the join code with a QR code, each part with its revealed questions and a “Reveal next question” control, live submission progress by group, the items approved for the projector one at a time, and discussion items. A Timer view shows the countdown full screen with start and pause controls. Keyboard: 1–6 switch views, ← → move between parts or items, R reveals the next question, F is full screen. A separate large-text page behind a secret, replaceable link is still available for a device you are not signed in on. Both receive only items approved for them, never drafts, hidden questions or private details; summaries and discussion questions appear only when separately switched on or revealed.

**Export.** JSON and CSV, identified or de-identified. Original participant work, the facilitator's setup, and AI/edited content are separate sections. Structured tables are preserved. Attachments are referenced by id, not embedded. CSV opens correctly in Excel (UTF-8 mark, formula-safe). No PINs, codes, tokens or keys are ever included.

**AI (optional).** Group summaries, cross-group comparison, and open-ended discussion questions, each with its own generate, review, edit and publish (or reveal) controls. The facilitator chooses the parts, groups, questions, submitted-only or drafts, and whether peer responses are included, sees what will be sent first (no names, class details, group names, PINs or recovery codes; people become “E7”, groups “Group B”), and every point must cite entries inside that selection; anything else is rejected. The model's original and the facilitator's edit are stored separately; drafts are private; the AI cannot change membership, release stages, publish, delete or see other sessions. A usage page shows requests and tokens, limits (per facilitator, set by an administrator) and a connection test.

## Run it on your own computer

Requires Node.js 22 or newer.

```bash
npm ci
npm test                 # 120 automated tests
npm run build
BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_EMAIL=you@example.org BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_NAME="You" BOOTSTRAP_ADMIN_PASSWORD="a-long-temporary-password" npm start
```
Open http://localhost:3000, sign in as a facilitator with those details, and choose a new password. Data is kept in `./data` (override with `DATA_DIR`). There are **no demo accounts**: the first administrator exists only if you supply the three BOOTSTRAP settings while there are no accounts.

Other commands: `npm run dev`, `npm run lint`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run backup`, `npm run restore -- <folder>`, `npm run routes`, `npm run schema`.

## How it is built (for whoever maintains it)

- Next.js 15 (App Router), TypeScript, Tailwind CSS. One catch-all API route (`app/api/[...path]/route.ts`) hands every request to a small router in `lib/router.ts`; handlers are in `lib/handlers/`.
- SQLite through `better-sqlite3`, one file, WAL mode. Schema changes are versioned, additive migrations in `lib/migrations.ts`; before pending migrations are applied to an existing database it is copied to `backups/`. Operations that must not race (joins and seat caps, submissions) run inside `BEGIN IMMEDIATE` transactions.
- Sign-in: facilitators use email + scrypt-hashed passwords; the first administrator comes from private settings; accounts are created by an administrator (no open sign-up) with a forced password change. Sessions are random tokens stored hashed, in `HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax` cookies (`Secure` when `COOKIE_SECURE=1`). Cross-site writes are refused and writes must be JSON.
- Guards: every route checks identity and ownership in code (`lib/authz.ts`, `lib/stages.ts`). Unknown or forbidden items answer “not found”. Throttling for logins, PINs, join codes and recovery codes is stored in the database, so it survives restarts.
- Files: type is decided from the file's own bytes (PNG/JPEG for participants; PNG/JPEG/WebP/PDF for facilitators), never from the name; random stored names with no extension; metadata stripped from participant images; limits (3 MB picture, 10 MB facilitator file) and quotas (100 MB per session, 25 pictures per participant); served only through permission-checked routes with `nosniff` and a sandboxing policy; PDFs are downloads; no SVG or HTML. Pictures that are never attached are removed after a day.
- Headers: a Content-Security-Policy, frame blocking, `nosniff`, a permissions policy, and HSTS. These are fixed when the app is **built**, so `COOKIE_SECURE=1` must already be set during the build for HSTS (Render does this from `render.yaml`).

## Data handling

- **Stored:** names, optional fields, entries (all versions), pictures, reactions, peer responses, submissions, shared items, AI drafts, AI usage details (time, type, size; never prompts or participant text), an audit history, and hashed sign-in records. The internet address is kept only inside short-lived throttling keys (cleared after a day).
- **Retention:** until the facilitator deletes the session. Deletion removes every record and file of that session and its audit history, leaving one line (who, when, id, counts). Backups made earlier keep their copy until you delete them.
- **Participant notice:** `/notice`, linked from the home and join pages. It states what is collected, who sees what, when content may go to an AI service, and how long it is kept. It does not claim legal or institutional compliance.

## What is and is not verified

See `docs/TEST-REPORT.md` for the detail. In short:

- **Automated tests (132):** editing questions and headings, sub-questions, timer, reaction icons, question reveal, recovery code viewed later, access control, joining, recovery, seat caps across processes, stage release, submission and versions, reopening, sharing and anonymity, projector content, reactions, peer responses, exports, uploads, the AI workflow with the simulator and with **mocked** HKU responses, usage limits, persistence across a restart, backup and restore, English and Traditional Chinese.
- **Checked by hand in a browser (Chromium):** the main screens work; automated accessibility checks (axe-core) found no violations on 20 page states after one fix; no sideways scrolling at phone width after fixing two overflows.
- **Rehearsed:** clean checkout, install, build, start, bootstrap, sign-in, restart, data still there.
- **NOT verified:** deployment on Render; any live request to the HKU service (only mocked); use with a screen reader, and keyboard-only use beyond what the automated checks cover; browsers other than Chromium; real phones; heavy load; an independent security review. WCAG 2.2 AA is the design target, **not a certification**.

## Known limitations and open decisions

- Real-time sync is polling (a few seconds), not push.
- The interface offers three stages; the database supports more.
- The reference panel shows whole earlier stages chosen by the facilitator, not individual contributions.
- Facilitators can hide an entry but cannot edit participants' words.
- One server instance only (SQLite is one file; an in-memory guard stops a double-clicked AI generation).
- No email: forgotten facilitator passwords are reset by an administrator.
- The group response box warns about unsaved text but does not block closing the tab (the entry editor does).
- AI: pictures and files are stored but not analysed; link retrieval is not implemented; there is no multi-step analysis for very long selections (they are refused with advice instead).
- Reactions are never used as evidence of correctness, and nothing grades participants.
- Decisions taken where the brief left a choice (change them if you disagree): a stage can return to “locked” only while it has no entries; “closed” wins over a reopened submission; participants cannot join before the session starts unless the facilitator allows it; a removed participant releases their seat; moving a participant leaves earlier entries in their original group; names on entries are as written at the time; AI limits apply to each facilitator; demo output may be shared after an explicit acknowledgement; group PINs are not copied when a session is duplicated.
